# Concept shortlist and the chosen episode (internal, not sent to the Director)

Test applied to every candidate: (a) is there a real narrative spine, not a list? (b) can it be
*drawn* — thought experiments, props, tableaux that explain rather than decorate? (c) do strong,
citable sources exist, including primary/institutional ones? (d) does it avoid re-treading the
reference's settled/contested claims (consciousness, death, free will, quantum mysticism)?

| # | Umbrella question | Spine? | Drawable? | Sources | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How old is the Earth — and how do you know?** | **Strong**: four clocks in sequence, each one breaking the last; ends in the present day | **Excellent**: strata, unconformity, cooling ball, hourglass, clean room, meteorite, ice core | Excellent: USGS, GSA Today (England/Molnar/Richter), Nature 1946, Patterson 1956, Royal Society/AC | **CHOSEN** |
| 2 | Why do we still not know how life began? | Good | Good (primordial soup, vents, RNA world) | Good (PNAS, Nature reviews) but genuinely unsettled with few crisp hooks | Rejected: risk of asserting contested chemistry as fact |
| 3 | What is time made of? | Weak for a film: collapses into relativity-and-philosophy montage | Hard: mostly abstract diagrams | Mixed: heavy physics papers + philosophical literature | Rejected: hard to stage characters in it |
| 4 | How did we work out what stars are made of? | Good (spectroscopy story) | Good (prism, spectra, eclipse expeditions) | Excellent | Rejected: strong runner-up, but ceiling overlaps with #1's "measure the unmeasurable" idea |
| 5 | Can you measure happiness? | Weak — survey data, no physical clock | Medium (questionnaires, scales) | Mixed: contested methodology | Rejected: weak visual causality, easy to slide into unsupported claims |
| 6 | Why is the sky dark at night (Olbers)? | Medium | Good (starfield, expanding space) | Good | Rejected: too short for 15 minutes without padding |

## Why #1 wins

1. **It has a plot with four acts and a reversal in each.** Ussher's book-clock → Hutton's
   *no number at all* → Kelvin's physical clock (brilliant, wrong planet) → Rutherford's
   nuclear clock (right idea, leaky container) → Patterson's clean-room cross-check. Each
   act breaks the thing the previous act trusted. That is a screen story, not a summary.
2. **Every abstract idea has a physical object**: a margin note, a genealogy list, a tilted
   outcrop, a cooling ball with heat leaving, an hourglass with a crack, a clean-room airlock,
   a mass spectrometer reading a ratio, a Greenland ice core.
3. **The payoff is honest and satisfying**: 4.54 ± 0.05 Ga, derived not from Earth but from
   iron meteorites — a genuinely counter-intuitive idea that viewers can leave with.
4. **It carries a real epilogue** (Patterson discovering that the lead ruining his samples was
   in everybody's blood), which converts a technical story into one with moral weight.
5. **It corrects a widely repeated error** (the "radioactivity fixed Kelvin" story) with a
   published geophysics critique — a chance to be right where most popular tellings are wrong.
6. **It supports a genuinely open question at the end** (biological ageing clocks) without
   pretending it is settled, which is the reference's own theme done honestly.

## Creative direction (chosen, not polled)

- **Visual grammar**: warm paper palette, ink line-work, situated interiors/landscapes, three
  distinct lighting moods (parchment day, workshop dusk, night/deep for the physics beats).
- **Narrative device**: the narrator is never on screen; a small recurring cast of the four
  clockmakers carries the story through costume and posture, plus a curator figure who poses
  the questions and appears at every chapter turn.
- **Typography**: Playpen Sans (display), Patrick Hand SC (caps labels), Caveat (marginalia),
  Public Sans (small supporting text). Nothing burned in except chapter cards and 1–5-word keys.
- **Audio**: one local neural voice (Kokoro, `am_michael`), an original synthesized score that
  changes key/mode per chapter and ducks under speech, plus paper/whoosh/clink cues at cards.
- **Camera**: scale changes and slow moves instead of cuts-as-decoration; hard cuts inside a
  chapter, single crossfades at chapter joins.
