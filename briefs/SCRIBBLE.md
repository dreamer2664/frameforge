## Pick a topic with a demonstrable useful payoff

Privately generate at least five viable topic/hook ideas and score their clarity, novelty, usefulness, source quality and drawability. Pick one yourself. A viewer should learn one concrete accurate distinction, usable everyday technique or surprising mechanism—not get a string of vague inspirational slogans. Avoid medical diagnosis, dangerous hacks, get-rich claims and topics that require heavy caveats to fit in a minute. If a myth needs debunking, make the actual limit clear.

Build a hook in the first 1–2 seconds, a concrete problem/conflict, an understandable visual explanation with escalating detail and a satisfying practical/curiosity payoff. Caption rules:
- Break into natural **2–6-word semantic phrases**; usually ≤2 lines. Large enough to read at phone size without covering acting or props. No tiny transcription paragraph or random per-word jumps.
- Measure actual spoken cues. Caption/highlight timing should follow real narration, normally within about 100–150 ms where that is measurable. Use actual cue durations or alignment, not guessed words-per-minute offsets.
- Highlight one useful keyword at a time using a disciplined accent colour/weight; avoid rainbow words and constant distracting bounce. Punctuation and intentional pause holds matter.
- Keep important text/face/props clear of typical mobile overlays: as a starting test, allow roughly top 180 px, bottom 300 px and right 160 px of extra risk space. Test the actual composition at phone size; do not treat those numbers as an eternal platform UI specification. Avoid clipping and disappearing captions during camera moves.
- Export accurate SRT/VTT as well. Burned-in captions are a designed narrative layer, not a replacement for a proper sidecar.

## Characters must explain through action

Aim for roughly **10–18 purposeful visual beats** and at least six materially different situations/compositions, adjusted to the story rather than a quota. Every meaningful claim needs visual causality: a desk/tool/task, an opposing character, a diagram, a simple experiment or a metaphor that clarifies rather than contradicts it. Change gestures, gaze, expression, staging, props and camera. Avoid a pile of unrelated image-search pictures above an idle mascot. At least one moment should pay off a setup visually, with minimal need for extra explanation.

Art may be original coded paths/drawings or rights-cleared assets; keep the style consistent. Do not copy the reference graduate mascot, album cover or photograph without legitimate rights. Use public figures only when the factual topic calls for them, with clear illustrative context; never clone their voice.

Choose narrator and delivery yourself. Test a few short local samples privately, choose energy/clarity/pronunciation that supports the hook, and fix difficult words before animating the whole cut. Light original/licensed music/SFX may help, but the voice must remain intelligible; no audio copied from TikTok.
